What Happens When an Employee Leaves Microsoft 365?

Deleting their account is not the first thing you should do. Here's how a sensible Microsoft 365 offboarding process protects access while preserving the business information you still need.

7 minute readMicrosoft 365SecurityJargon-free advice
1

First: Stop the Person Signing In

When an employee leaves, the first priority is protecting company access.

Their Microsoft 365 sign-in should be blocked and active access dealt with as part of a controlled offboarding process.

This prevents the former employee continuing to access company email, files and other Microsoft 365 services using their old account.

2

Don't Just Delete the Account Immediately

Deleting the account may sound like the obvious first step, but the business may still need the person's email, OneDrive files, contacts or other information.

Secure first. Preserve what the business needs. Delete later.

A good leaver process decides what should happen to the data before the account is finally removed.

3

What Happens to Their Email?

The business may need to keep receiving messages sent to the former employee.

Depending on the requirement, administrators can arrange appropriate access, forwarding or convert a mailbox to a shared mailbox before final account deletion.

This can help colleagues continue dealing with customers and suppliers who still use the old address.

4

What Happens to Their OneDrive Files?

OneDrive is associated with the individual user, so important business files should be reviewed before the account is removed.

Microsoft provides administrators with ways to give another employee access to a former user's OneDrive data so required information can be preserved or moved.

This is also why shared company documents are generally better placed in SharePoint rather than being left permanently in one employee's OneDrive.

5

What About Files They Put in SharePoint?

SharePoint files belong to the SharePoint site rather than the employee's personal OneDrive.

Removing the user does not mean the company's SharePoint documents disappear with them.

The important task is reviewing permissions and ownership responsibilities where necessary.

6

What About Their Laptop or Phone?

Company devices should be recovered and access reviewed as part of offboarding.

Where devices are centrally managed, administrators may have additional options for retiring devices or removing company data.

The exact action depends on whether the equipment belongs to the business, the employee and how it has been managed.

7

Can We Reuse Their Microsoft 365 Licence?

Yes. Once the account and data have been dealt with appropriately, the Microsoft 365 licence can normally be removed and assigned to another user if the subscription allows it.

But licence removal and user deletion should not be treated as the entire leaver process. The business data and ongoing email requirements need to be considered first.

8

How Long Is Deleted User Data Kept?

Microsoft currently provides a recovery window after a user is deleted. Microsoft documents a 30-day period in which a deleted user can be restored before permanent deletion.

Retention can vary depending on the service, configuration and any additional retention or compliance policies the organisation uses.

Do not treat the recovery window as your backup strategy. Important business information should be deliberately preserved before deletion rather than relying on the ability to recover it later.
9

A Sensible Microsoft 365 Leaver Process

  • ✓Block the former employee's access
  • ✓Review their mailbox and future incoming email
  • ✓Preserve required OneDrive and business data
  • ✓Recover or manage company devices
  • ✓Review shared access and permissions
  • ✓Remove or reassign licences at the appropriate point
  • ✓Delete the account only when the business is ready
10

The CloudMonsta View

A leaver should be treated as a small IT process, not simply a request to delete a username.

The goal is to remove the person's access quickly while keeping the information the business still owns and needs.

Having a standard offboarding checklist makes that process repeatable and greatly reduces the chance of losing useful data or leaving unnecessary access behind.
