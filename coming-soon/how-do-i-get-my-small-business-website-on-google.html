How Do I Get My Small Business Website on Google?

Getting a website online is only the first step. Here's how Google discovers, understands and indexes a small-business site.

6 minute readBusiness websitesWebsitesJargon-free advice
1

First, Make Sure Google Can Reach It

Your site needs to be publicly accessible over the web and should not accidentally block search engines through robots settings or page-level indexing directives.

Keep it practical: the right Microsoft 365 or website setup should solve a real business need, not add complexity for its own sake.
2

Give Every Important Page a Clear Purpose

Pages should clearly explain the service or subject they cover. Useful headings, readable text and descriptive page titles help both customers and search engines understand the content.

3

Create a Sitemap

An XML sitemap provides search engines with a list of important URLs on the website and can help them discover new and updated pages.

4

Use Google Search Console

Search Console lets a site owner submit a sitemap, inspect URLs and see information about how Google is crawling and indexing the site.

5

Request Indexing for Important New Pages

URL Inspection can be useful after publishing an important new or significantly updated page, although requesting indexing does not guarantee immediate inclusion.

6

Make the Site Useful, Not Just Search-Friendly

Google's systems are designed to surface useful content. Write pages for real customers first rather than filling them with repetitive keywords.

7

Local Businesses Should Think Local

Make your service area and business details clear where relevant, and maintain an accurate Google Business Profile if the business is eligible.

8

Internal Links Help

Link related pages together naturally so visitors and crawlers can discover useful content across the site.

9

Be Patient

New sites and pages can take time to be discovered, crawled and indexed. Search visibility normally develops over time rather than appearing the moment a site goes live.

✓

The CloudMonsta View

Good foundations beat SEO tricks: a technically accessible site, useful service pages, a sitemap, Search Console and genuinely helpful content give a small business somewhere sensible to start.
