Do I Need Microsoft 365 Business Premium?

Is Business Premium worth the extra cost? A practical guide to when Intune, Conditional Access and Defender for Business make sense.

6 minute readMicrosoft 365SecurityJargon-free advice
1

What Is Business Premium?

Microsoft 365 Business Premium combines the familiar Microsoft 365 productivity services with additional identity, device-management and endpoint-security capabilities. The important difference is not simply more Office features; it is the extra control around users, devices and company information.

Keep it practical: the right Microsoft 365 or website setup should solve a real business need, not add complexity for its own sake.
2

What Do You Get Beyond Business Standard?

Business Premium adds capabilities such as Microsoft Intune, Microsoft Entra ID Plan 1 features including Conditional Access, and Microsoft Defender for Business. These can help a company manage devices, control access and improve endpoint protection.

3

When Does Premium Start to Make Sense?

Premium becomes more compelling when employees work remotely, use company laptops, handle sensitive information, or the business wants consistent security rather than configuring every device individually.

4

What Does Intune Add?

Intune provides cloud-based management for supported laptops, phones, tablets and applications. It can apply policies, deploy apps and help determine whether managed devices meet company requirements.

5

What Does Conditional Access Add?

Conditional Access lets the organisation make smarter access decisions. Instead of relying only on a password, policies can consider factors such as the user, application, authentication and device state.

6

What Does Defender for Business Add?

Defender for Business adds centrally managed endpoint protection, detection and response capabilities. This gives the business greater visibility than treating each computer as an isolated antivirus installation.

7

Does Everyone Need Premium?

Not always. Microsoft 365 licences are assigned per user, so a business can choose the appropriate licence for different roles where licensing terms and technical requirements allow.

8

When Might Standard Be Enough?

If users mainly need professional email, cloud storage and desktop Office applications, and the business does not require the additional management and security capabilities, Business Standard may still be appropriate.

9

Is Premium Worth It for a Small Company?

Company size alone is not the deciding factor. A five-person company handling valuable customer data may benefit more from central security than a larger organisation with simpler requirements.

✓

The CloudMonsta View

Business Premium is worth considering when Microsoft 365 has become central to how the company works and protecting users and devices matters as much as providing email and Office applications.
