What Is MFA and Why Does My Business Need It?

Multi-factor authentication adds another check when somebody signs in. Here's why that matters when passwords are stolen.

6 minute readSecurityMicrosoft 365Jargon-free advice
1

What Is MFA?

Multi-factor authentication asks for more than one form of verification when a user signs in. A password is one factor; an additional approved verification method provides another.

Keep it practical: the right Microsoft 365 or website setup should solve a real business need, not add complexity for its own sake.
2

Why Isn't a Password Enough?

Passwords can be guessed, reused, phished or stolen. If a criminal obtains the correct password, password-only protection may give them exactly what they need.

3

What Does MFA Change?

With MFA, knowing the password alone may not be enough to complete the sign-in. That creates an additional barrier for an attacker.

4

What Does MFA Look Like for Employees?

Depending on the organisation's configuration, users may verify sign-ins using an authenticator application or another supported method. The exact experience depends on the authentication policy.

5

Will Users Be Prompted Every Time?

Not necessarily. Sign-in behaviour depends on the organisation's security configuration, the application, session and risk context.

6

Is MFA Annoying?

There is a small amount of additional setup, but modern authentication methods can make normal sign-ins straightforward. The security benefit is significant compared with relying on passwords alone.

7

Does MFA Stop Every Attack?

No security control is perfect. Users can still be targeted by sophisticated phishing and social-engineering attacks, which is why MFA should be part of a wider security approach.

8

What About Administrator Accounts?

Privileged accounts deserve especially strong protection because compromising an administrator can give an attacker much broader control over the environment.

9

Where Does Conditional Access Fit?

Conditional Access can apply more detailed rules around when and how authentication is required, particularly with suitable Microsoft 365 licensing.

✓

The CloudMonsta View

MFA is one of the most important baseline protections a small business can put around Microsoft 365 accounts. A password should not be the only thing standing between an attacker and company data.
