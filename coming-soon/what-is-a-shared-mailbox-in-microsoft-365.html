What Is a Shared Mailbox in Microsoft 365?

A simple guide to shared mailboxes such as accounts@, sales@ and support@ — and why sharing one person's password is the wrong approach.

6 minute readBusiness emailMicrosoft 365Jargon-free advice
1

What Is a Shared Mailbox?

A shared mailbox is an Exchange Online mailbox designed so authorised people can read and send messages from a common business address such as accounts@, sales@ or support@.

Keep it practical: the right Microsoft 365 or website setup should solve a real business need, not add complexity for its own sake.
2

Why Use One?

Shared mailboxes separate a business function from one employee. Customers can contact a consistent address even when staff change, and several authorised users can work with the same mailbox.

3

Do Staff Share a Password?

No. Users should sign in with their own Microsoft 365 accounts and be granted permission to the shared mailbox. That is much safer and easier to manage than giving several people one password.

4

Can People Send From the Shared Address?

Yes, appropriate permissions can allow users to send as the shared mailbox or send on its behalf, depending on how the organisation wants messages to appear.

5

Does a Shared Mailbox Need a Licence?

Microsoft permits shared mailboxes without a separate licence in common scenarios, subject to service limits and feature requirements. Some configurations, larger storage requirements or advanced features can require licensing.

6

What Happens When Someone Leaves?

Because users access the mailbox through their own accounts, access can be removed from the departing employee without changing a shared password for everybody else.

7

Shared Mailbox or Distribution List?

A distribution list mainly distributes incoming messages to members. A shared mailbox provides a common mailbox where authorised users can work with the same email history and send from the shared address.

8

Good Uses for Shared Mailboxes

Common examples include accounts@, enquiries@, sales@, support@ and bookings@ — addresses representing a business function rather than one individual.

9

Keep Permissions Sensible

Only people who genuinely need the mailbox should have access. Periodically reviewing membership helps prevent former staff or changed roles retaining unnecessary access.

✓

The CloudMonsta View

If several people need to use one business address, give each person their own identity and use a shared mailbox. Do not solve the problem by sharing one user's password.
