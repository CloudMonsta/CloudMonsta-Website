Why Does My Business Need SSL/HTTPS?

HTTPS encrypts the connection between a visitor and your website. Here's why every modern business website should use it.

6 minute readBusiness websitesWebsitesJargon-free advice
1

What Is HTTPS?

HTTPS is the secure version of HTTP, the protocol used when browsers communicate with websites. It uses TLS to protect the connection between the visitor and the web server.

Keep it practical: the right Microsoft 365 or website setup should solve a real business need, not add complexity for its own sake.
2

What Does the Certificate Do?

A TLS certificate helps the browser establish an encrypted connection and verify the site identity associated with the certificate.

3

Why Does Encryption Matter?

Encryption helps prevent information travelling between the browser and website from being read or altered in transit by parties who should not have access to it.

4

Is HTTPS Only Needed for Online Shops?

No. Even simple brochure websites should use HTTPS. Contact forms, logins and other interactions make secure transport particularly important, but HTTPS is now a normal baseline for modern sites.

5

What Will Customers See?

Modern browsers expect secure websites. Visitors may receive warnings or see negative security indicators when a site is served insecurely.

6

Does HTTPS Make the Whole Website Secure?

No. HTTPS protects data in transit; it does not fix vulnerable code, weak passwords, outdated software or poor hosting security.

7

What About Search Engines?

Google has long used HTTPS as a ranking signal, but the more important reason to enable it is providing visitors with a secure connection.

8

Do Certificates Have to Be Expensive?

Not necessarily. Many modern hosting services include and automatically manage TLS certificates for custom domains.

9

What About Azure Static Web Apps?

Platforms such as Azure Static Web Apps can provide HTTPS for hosted sites and custom domains, reducing the need for a small business to manually manage a web-server certificate.

✓

The CloudMonsta View

HTTPS should be treated as standard website plumbing, not an optional upgrade. Every professional business website should provide visitors with a secure connection.
