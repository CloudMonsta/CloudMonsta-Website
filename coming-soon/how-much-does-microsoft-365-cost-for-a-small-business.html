How Much Does Microsoft 365 Cost for a Small Business?

Microsoft 365 costs more than the licence alone. Here's what a small business should budget for when planning email, migration, security, backup and support.

6 minute readMicrosoft 365LicensingJargon-free advice
1

Start With the Licence

The recurring Microsoft licence is the most obvious cost. Business Basic, Business Standard and Business Premium provide different levels of applications, security and device management, so the correct plan depends on how each employee works.

Keep it practical: the right Microsoft 365 or website setup should solve a real business need, not add complexity for its own sake.
2

You Don't Necessarily Need One Licence Type

A business may be able to mix licence types for different users. Someone who mainly needs email may have different requirements from an employee who needs desktop Office applications or managed company devices.

3

Allow for Initial Setup

A new Microsoft 365 environment needs more than licences. Domains, users, mailboxes, security settings and the basic company structure need to be configured correctly.

4

Migration Can Be a Separate Cost

Moving existing email and files from another provider can require planning, data migration, testing and user support. The amount of data and number of users both affect the work involved.

5

Security Can Change the Licence Choice

If the business wants Intune, Conditional Access and Defender for Business, Business Premium may be more appropriate than adding disconnected security products later.

6

What About Backup?

Microsoft 365 is a cloud service, but businesses should still decide how they want to protect their own email and files. A managed backup service creates an additional ongoing cost but addresses a different requirement from the Microsoft 365 licence itself.

7

Ongoing Support and Administration

Some businesses manage Microsoft 365 internally. Others prefer ongoing help with users, licences, security, troubleshooting and routine administration. That support should be considered separately from Microsoft's subscription charge.

8

Avoid Buying on Price Alone

The cheapest plan can become poor value if employees cannot do their jobs properly. Equally, buying Premium for every user without a reason can create unnecessary cost.

9

Think About Total Cost, Not Just Per-User Price

A realistic budget considers licences, initial setup, migration, backup, ongoing management and any project work rather than comparing Microsoft 365 only on a monthly licence figure.

✓

The CloudMonsta View

Start with requirements, not a price list. Work out what each user needs, what data must move, how devices will be secured and what ongoing support the business expects; the cost then becomes much easier to understand.
