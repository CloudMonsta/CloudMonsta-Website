What Is a Domain Name and How Does It Work?

What is a domain, who owns it, and how does it connect to your website and business email? Here's the simple version.

6 minute readBusiness websitesWebsitesJargon-free advice
1

What Is a Domain Name?

A domain name is the human-friendly address people use to find a business online, such as example.co.uk or example.com.

Keep it practical: the right Microsoft 365 or website setup should solve a real business need, not add complexity for its own sake.
2

Is the Domain the Website?

No. The domain is the address. The website is content hosted on a web service. DNS connects the domain name to the service that should answer when somebody visits it.

3

Who Owns the Domain?

A domain is registered through a domain registrar for a defined registration period. The business should keep control of the registrar account and ensure renewals are maintained.

4

What Is DNS?

DNS is often described as the internet's address book. It contains records that direct different uses of your domain towards the correct services.

5

How Does Email Use the Same Domain?

DNS can direct web traffic to one provider and email to another. This is how a business can host its website in one place while Microsoft 365 handles email.

6

What Is an MX Record?

An MX record tells other mail systems where email for your domain should be delivered.

7

What Are A and CNAME Records?

These are common DNS record types used to direct names towards web services and other destinations. The exact record needed depends on the hosting platform.

8

Can I Change Website Provider Without Changing Domain?

Usually, yes. You can keep the same domain and update DNS so it points to a new hosting service.

9

Protect the Registrar Account

Because the domain controls important parts of the business's online identity, protect the registrar login with strong authentication and keep recovery details current.

✓

The CloudMonsta View

Your domain is one of your business's key digital assets. Keep ownership and renewal under your control, then use DNS to connect it cleanly to your website, Microsoft 365 and other services.
