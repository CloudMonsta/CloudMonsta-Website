10 Signs Your Business Has Outgrown Gmail

Gmail can be a great place to start, but growing businesses often reach a point where email, files, users and security need more structure. Here are ten signs it may be time to consider Microsoft 365.

Gmail May Not Be the Problem

Gmail is a great email platform, and for many small businesses it is where business communication begins.

A new company may start with a personal Gmail address, a handful of separate accounts, or a simple email setup that works perfectly well while the business is small.

But as a business grows, the way it communicates, stores information and manages users usually becomes more complicated.

At some point, email is no longer just email. It becomes part of how your team communicates, shares files, holds meetings, protects company information and manages access when people join or leave.

Here are ten signs your business may have reached that point.

1

You're Still Using an @gmail.com Address

An address such as yourbusiness@gmail.com may have been perfectly adequate when you started.

But customers increasingly expect established businesses to communicate from their own domain, such as:

name@yourbusiness.com

A professional domain-based email address helps reinforce your company identity and can make your business appear more established and trustworthy.

Microsoft 365 allows you to use your existing business domain for email while managing your mailboxes centrally through Exchange Online.

2

Everyone Manages Their Own Email Account

When a business only has one or two people, individually managed accounts may not cause many problems.

As the team grows, however, you need to know who has access to company systems and be able to manage that access centrally.

A proper Microsoft 365 environment gives your business a central place to create users, assign licences, reset passwords and control access.

That becomes particularly important when employees join or leave.

3

You're Sharing Passwords

Shared passwords are often a sign that a business has outgrown its original email setup.

Perhaps several people need access to:

sales@yourbusiness.com

or

accounts@yourbusiness.com

Sharing the password may seem like the easiest solution, but it makes security and accountability much harder.

Microsoft 365 supports shared mailboxes, allowing authorised employees to access addresses such as Sales, Accounts or Support using their own individual accounts.

No shared password is required.

4

Files Are Scattered Everywhere

As businesses grow, documents often end up spread across laptops, email attachments, personal cloud storage and various folders.

Eventually nobody is completely sure which copy is the latest version.

Microsoft 365 provides OneDrive and SharePoint for business file storage and collaboration.

Instead of repeatedly emailing documents backwards and forwards, your team can work from centrally stored files with controlled access and version history.

5

Your Team Is Constantly Emailing Files to Each Other

Email attachments work, but they quickly become inefficient when several people are working on the same document.

You can easily end up with files called:

Proposal-Final.docx
Proposal-Final-v2.docx
Proposal-Final-REALLY-FINAL.docx

With Microsoft 365, Word, Excel and PowerPoint documents can be stored in OneDrive or SharePoint and worked on collaboratively.

Multiple people can even work on the same document without creating endless duplicate copies.

6

You Use Several Separate Apps to Run the Business

Perhaps you use one service for email, another for video meetings, another for cloud storage and something else for team messaging.

There is nothing inherently wrong with using different services.

But as the number of systems grows, so does the number of accounts, passwords, subscriptions and places where company information is stored.

Microsoft 365 brings many everyday business tools together under one account, including:

  • ✓Outlook for email and calendars
  • ✓Teams for communication and meetings
  • ✓OneDrive for individual business files
  • ✓SharePoint for shared company information
  • ✓Word, Excel and PowerPoint depending on your licence

For a growing business, having these services working together can make administration considerably simpler.

7

You're Worried About Email Security

Email remains one of the most common ways attackers target businesses.

Phishing, stolen passwords, spoofed emails and malicious attachments can affect businesses of any size.

A growing business should therefore be thinking about more than simply having a strong password.

Microsoft 365 can provide features such as multi-factor authentication, security policies and additional threat protection depending on the licence you choose.

Your business domain can also be configured with SPF, DKIM and DMARC to help protect against email spoofing and improve the trustworthiness of messages sent from your domain.

8

Someone Leaving the Business Would Cause a Headache

Ask yourself a simple question:

If somebody left tomorrow, could you quickly remove their access while keeping the business information you need?

If the answer is no, your current setup may be becoming difficult to manage.

With Microsoft 365, user accounts and access can be centrally administered.

A departing employee's access can be removed while appropriate business information can be retained or transferred according to your requirements.

That is much safer than relying on passwords being handed back or hoping someone remembers every service the employee could access.

9

Remote Working Has Become Normal

Even very small businesses now regularly work from different locations.

Employees may need to access email, documents and company information from the office, home or while travelling.

Microsoft 365 is designed around cloud-based working.

Employees can access authorised resources across supported devices while the business retains much greater control over identities, information and security.

Microsoft Teams also provides a central location for meetings, chat and collaboration.

10

Your IT Setup No Longer Feels Simple

This is often the biggest sign.

What began as a simple email account has gradually become:

  • ✓Multiple users
  • ✓Shared addresses
  • ✓Cloud storage
  • ✓Calendars and meetings
  • ✓File sharing
  • ✓Security concerns
  • ✓Lots of different passwords

Nothing may be completely broken.

It has simply become harder to manage.

That's usually the point where moving to a properly structured business platform starts to make sense.

?

So, Should You Move From Gmail to Microsoft 365?

Not every business using Gmail needs to migrate.

If you're a sole trader with a simple setup and Gmail does everything you need, there may be no urgent reason to change.

But if several of the signs above sound familiar, Microsoft 365 can provide a much stronger foundation for future growth.

The important part isn't simply replacing Gmail with Outlook.

A well-planned Microsoft 365 setup can give your business professional email, centralised user management, shared mailboxes, file collaboration, Teams, improved security and a platform that can grow alongside the company.

→

Moving Doesn't Have to Mean Losing Everything

One concern businesses often have is what will happen to their existing email and files.

A Microsoft 365 migration can be planned so that existing business email and data are moved into the new environment, with the changeover carefully managed to minimise disruption.

The exact approach depends on your current setup, the number of users, how much data you have and where that information is currently stored.
