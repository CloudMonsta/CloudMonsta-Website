Does My Small Business Really Need a Website?

If most of your customers already find you through Facebook, Instagram or word of mouth, is a website really necessary? For many small businesses, the answer is yes — but probably not for the reasons you think.

7 minute read Business Websites Small Business Jargon-free advice
!

You May Already Be Getting Customers Without One

Plenty of small businesses operate successfully without a traditional website.

You may already get enquiries through Facebook, Instagram, Google Business Profile, local recommendations or word of mouth.

If that is working, it is reasonable to ask why you should spend money on a website at all.

The answer is not that every business needs a huge website with dozens of pages.

The real value is having a professional online home that you control, where potential customers can quickly understand who you are, what you offer and how to contact you.

1

It Makes Your Business Look More Established

Customers often check a business online before making contact.

Even when somebody has been recommended to you, they may still search your company name to see what they can find.

A well-designed website can instantly show that your business is genuine, active and professional.

It also gives you somewhere to present your logo, services, contact details, testimonials and company information consistently.

Think of your website as your digital shop window. It does not need to be enormous. It just needs to give people confidence that they have found the right business.
2

You Own Your Website — You Don't Own Social Media

Facebook and Instagram can be excellent marketing tools, but they are still somebody else's platforms.

The platform controls the layout, algorithm, rules and how much of your content people actually see.

Your account could also be restricted, hacked or temporarily unavailable.

Your own website gives your business a permanent online presence that you control.

You can decide what appears, how it is presented and where customers are directed next.

3

It Gives Google More to Find

A website gives search engines more information about your business, your services and the areas you serve.

That creates opportunities for customers to discover you when they search for something you provide rather than searching for your business name specifically.

A Facebook page can appear in search results, but a proper website gives you much more control over the pages, wording and information Google can understand.

4

You Can Explain Your Services Properly

Social posts move down a feed quickly.

A website allows you to give each important service a permanent home.

You can explain:

  • ✓What you offer
  • ✓Who the service is for
  • ✓What is included
  • ✓How the process works
  • ✓Typical pricing or starting points
  • ✓How customers can get started

That means potential customers can answer many of their own questions before they contact you.

5

Your Website Works When You Aren't Working

A website is available 24 hours a day.

Someone can discover your business in the evening, look through your services, read your FAQs and send an enquiry without waiting for you to answer the phone.

For service businesses, that can be particularly useful because customers often research options outside normal working hours.

Your website can continue introducing the business even while you are busy doing the actual work.

6

It Gives Customers More Reasons to Trust You

Trust is especially important when customers are choosing a small business they have not used before.

Your website can bring together information that would otherwise be scattered across different platforms.

  • ✓Customer reviews and testimonials
  • ✓Case studies
  • ✓Examples of previous work
  • ✓Professional qualifications or certifications
  • ✓Insurance or relevant credentials
  • ✓Clear contact and business information

Individually, these may seem small. Together, they help reassure someone who is deciding whether to contact you.

7

It Can Make Enquiries Easier

Your website does not need to simply display a phone number.

It can provide clear ways for customers to take the next step.

Depending on your business, that could include:

  • ✓A contact form
  • ✓A quote request
  • ✓Online bookings
  • ✓A consultation request
  • ✓Links to email, phone or WhatsApp

A good website makes the next action obvious rather than making the customer hunt around for contact details.

8

You Can Show Off Your Work Properly

For many small businesses, photographs are one of the strongest selling tools available.

A builder, gardener, hairdresser, dog groomer, tattoo artist, craft business or venue can show potential customers exactly what they do.

Social media is useful for this too, but a website allows you to organise your best work rather than relying on customers scrolling through months of posts.

You can create a gallery, portfolio, case studies or dedicated pages for different types of work.

9

A Website and Social Media Work Better Together

A website does not replace Facebook or Instagram.

For many businesses, the best approach is to use both.

Social media is excellent for regular updates, conversations, offers and showing that the business is active.

Your website provides the permanent information underneath it all.

Social media gets attention.

Your website helps turn that attention into an enquiry.

You can link social posts back to specific service pages, offers, booking forms or useful articles on your own site.

10

Your Website Doesn't Need to Be Huge

One reason small businesses avoid websites is the assumption that they need something complicated.

They often do not.

A straightforward business website may only need:

  • ✓A strong homepage
  • ✓Your main services
  • ✓About the business
  • ✓Photos, examples or testimonials
  • ✓Frequently asked questions
  • ✓A clear contact or booking option

For many small businesses, five or six well-written pages are far more useful than a large website filled with content nobody reads.

?

Does Every Small Business Need One?

Not necessarily.

If you have more work than you can handle, rely entirely on repeat customers and have no interest in attracting new enquiries, a website may not be a priority.

Likewise, a brand-new business may decide to start with a simple social presence while it proves the idea.

But if you want customers to find you, understand your services, trust your business and contact you more easily, a website can be one of the most useful foundations you put in place.

The CloudMonsta View

A small-business website should not exist simply because somebody says every company needs one.

It should have a job to do.

That might be generating enquiries, explaining your services, supporting existing customers, taking bookings or simply giving people confidence that your business is professional and legitimate.

The best small-business websites are usually simple, clear and focused on what the customer actually wants to know.
